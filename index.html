📖 LIS211 — Information Literacy

1. Meaning of Information Literacy

Information literacy goes beyond simply finding information online or in books.

It involves knowing when information is needed, where to find it, how to evaluate it, how to organize it, how to use it ethically and how to create new knowledge from it.

2. Components of Information Literacy

Important components include:

• Identifying information needs
• Locating information
• Evaluating information
• Organizing information
• Using information ethically
• Creating new knowledge

3. Identifying Information Needs

An information need is the gap between what a person already knows and what the person needs to know.

Information may be needed to solve a problem, answer a question, make a decision or complete a task.

When identifying an information need, consider the scope, key concepts, questions and the depth or type of information required.

4. Locating Information

A good information user should understand the strengths and limitations of different information sources.

Examples of academic information databases include:

Scopus
Web of Science
JSTOR
EBSCO

5. Evaluating Information

Information should be evaluated before it is used. Important considerations include:

• Credibility
• Relevance
• Authority
• Accuracy
• Currency
• Evidence
• Purpose

6. CRAAP Test

The CRAAP Test is used to evaluate information sources.

C — Currency
R — Relevance
A — Authority
A — Accuracy
P — Purpose

7. Organizing Information

Organizing information refers to the systematic arrangement, classification, documentation and management of information collected from different sources so that it can be understood, evaluated and reused.

It helps a student to synthesize evidence, identify relationships, compare different perspectives and construct coherent arguments.

8. Ethical Use of Information

Information should be used responsibly and ethically. Users should acknowledge their sources, respect copyright and avoid plagiarism.

9. Creating New Knowledge

Information literacy also involves using information through analysis and synthesis to develop new understanding or knowledge.
